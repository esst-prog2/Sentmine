## Problem Statement

sentmine needs a promo page on GitHub Pages (Homework 9, due 2026-11-14 20:00) that tells a stranger what it is for, who it is for, and how to try it. The reader is a student who reads English course books, as the author does (statistics and causal inference), and who meets words and terms they want to remember. Today that student copies sentences into Anki by hand: the author timed 10 cards at about 3 minutes by hand against about 1 minute in the first app (PLANNING_LOG, 2026-10-09), so most words never become cards.

## What Makes This Cool

- You read the book as it looks. One click on a printed word gives a card made from its sentence; one more click suggests what it means.
- Your cards come back while you read. In the next chapter, words and terms you already have cards for are underlined, pointing at one shows your own card, and each card counts how many texts you met it again in. In the reader-to-Anki tools found in a web search on 2026-11-14 (Pick2anki, Vocabsieve, browser add-ons), nothing like this was described; they were not tried by hand.
- A definition you drag over becomes a cloze card that hides the term in its own sentence.

## Constraints

- Due today, 20:00 (half credit until Monday 16 November, 20:00). The address of the page is what gets submitted.
- Content with gstack `/office-hours` (this session); the look with Impeccable `shape`, `generate`, `critique`; published on GitHub Pages; `PROCESS.md` row and commit per step.
- GitHub Pages publishes everything in the served folder. The page lives in `docs/`, which holds only the page and its pictures; nothing personal goes in it.
- Every screenshot comes from a real run; every number has a source (course rule, session 8 slides).
- sentmine runs on the reader's own computer; there is no online version to link.
- The course book is copyrighted: screenshots show parts of its pages, and the file is not in the repository.

## Recommended Approach

Approach B. The page follows the same five steps as the December talk (as the professor advised for the talk on 2026-11-10), so the page, the 30-second video (Homework 10) and the five slides share one story and one set of real screenshots.

Wireframe: none (the look is made with Impeccable `shape` and `generate`, as Homework 9 requires).

Page content, in order. The wording below is the draft the look is built around; Impeccable may shorten it, not change its claims.

1. **Opening.** Headline: "Read the real page. Click a word. It's an Anki card." One line under it: "sentmine is a reading app for students who read English course books. It turns the words and terms you meet into Anki cards, and brings those cards back when you meet them again." Screenshot 1.
2. **Five steps, one screenshot and one sentence each.**
   1. Click a word on the PDF page: a card with the sentence it came from (`docs/img/1-click-a-word.png`).
   2. Suggest meaning: a short meaning from `gpt-5-nano`, for you to check (`docs/img/2-suggest-meaning.png`).
   3. Drag over a term, then its definition: a cloze card that hides the term (`docs/img/3-cloze-card.png`).
   4. Send to Anki: in your deck in about 5 seconds (screenshot 4, to take with Anki open).
   5. Meet the word again: clicking `simultaneously` in a later study question shows sentmine's own notice `'simultaneously' already has a card from DATE`, with your earlier card, Skip and Make it anyway (screenshot 5, to take; not Anki's duplicate message).
3. **"Your cards come back while you read."** Underlined words and terms, your card when you point at one, and "Met again in N texts" on My cards (screenshot 6, to take on the same real run).
4. **Why I built it.** Copying sentences into Anki by hand was slow, so most words never became cards. The first version guessed my new words from a list of words I know: of 103 cards it made on one Guardian article, 4 were really new to me (Homework 4, linked to the README's section on it and to `PLANNING_LOG.md` in the repository). So now I choose, while reading.
5. **Numbers, each with its source.**
   - 10 cards: Download and import 30 s; Send to Anki 5 s. One timing by hand (`PLANNING_LOG.md`, entry of 2026-11-03, linked).
   - Words found on the PDF pages: 99.9 to 100% on four real PDFs (`PLANNING_LOG.md`, 2026-11-06, linked).
   - 555 automated tests (`pytest`, run on 2026-11-14; the tests folder is linked).
   Each number on the page links to where it comes from in the repository.
6. **Similar tools.** One honest paragraph: Pick2anki, Vocabsieve and browser add-ons also send words to Anki. In what a web search found about them (2026-11-14, not tried by hand), cloze cards from definitions and cards coming back while you read were not described; sentmine does both.
7. **Try it.** Python 3.10 or newer; `git clone https://github.com/esst-prog2/Sentmine`, `cd Sentmine`, `pip install .`, `sentmine`. Anki with the AnkiConnect add-on is optional (Download works without it). Suggest meaning needs your own OpenAI key: set `OPENAI_API_KEY`, or put the line `OPENAI_API_KEY=...` in `.env` in sentmine's user folder (`%APPDATA%\sentmine` on Windows). It runs on your own computer and your text stays there, except one thing: when you press Suggest meaning, that word and its sentence are sent to OpenAI.
8. **What's next.** Read anywhere in the browser: pick words on web pages, and send PDFs from Canvas straight into sentmine.
9. **Footer.** A project for Advanced Programming at ELTE. Link to the repository. A place for the video (Homework 10) and, later, the slides.
