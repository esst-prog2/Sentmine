4,640 Nouns and 18 Noun Suffixes: Giant List of Untouchable Nouns

We will cover a lot on this page, and you will leave with a fantastic newfound understanding of nouns. You will want to spend some time with the giant list of 4,640 nouns that follow, and you will want to gain a solid understanding of the list of 18 noun suffixes that follow. But before all that, we need some basic but unusual noun knowledge.

Truthfully, much of what we teach young students about grammar and language is wrong. Take a look at these two sentences:

  1.  We demand excellence!    (excellence = noun).
  2.  This is excellent!    (excellent = adjective).

Do you find that the adjective is any more descriptive than the noun? I don’t. In fact, to most people, the two words mean about the same thing. Semantically, they are very similar. But syntactically, they are different because they function as different parts of speech. Let’s take a closer look at this.

The Noun Slot: Where Do Nouns Go in a Sentence?

Syntax is the set of rules that guide where words belong in a sentence. In short, sentences are composed of syntactic slots. Adjectives go in the adjective slot, nouns go in the noun slot, etc.

We have five basic noun slots: 1) subject, 2) predicate noun, 3) direct object, 4) indirect object, and 5) object of the preposition. Can you identify all of the nouns in the sentence below? (Sadly, most people can’t. Congratulations if you can! I provide the answers below!)

»  The juxtaposition of opposites is an effective strategy for creating interest.

Before we take a closer look at that sentence, here are a few simpler sentences that highlight the noun slots:

1.   Reading (subject) is an essential skill (predicate noun).
2.   Sue (subject) bought a present (direct object) for her mom (indirect object).
3.   Bill (subject) leaves for school (object of the preposition) early in the morning (object of the preposition).

Now, here is a closer examination of that sentence from above:

»  The juxtaposition (subject) of opposites (object of the preposition) is an effective strategy (predicate noun) for creating (object of the preposition) interest (direct object).

Many of the nouns on the giant list of 4,640 nouns more closely resemble the complex nouns in the above sentence compared to the simpler nouns in the sentences above. Let’s take a closer look at these complex nouns.

Mostly Untouchable: Touchable Nouns Omitted

If you scroll down, you will find a massive list of nouns. Interestingly, most of the nouns on the list are UNTOUCHABLE NOUNS. Yes, I intentionally omitted most touchable nouns, and in the process, I left out most of the nouns that young students typically learn. We can’t keep teaching kids the same kinds of nouns year after year and expect them to master how language really works.

Concrete vs. Abstract Nouns VS. Touchable vs. Untouchable Nouns

Teachers often teach concrete and abstract nouns in the context of touchable and untouchable. (Note: It’s a bit more complicated than this.) My goal was to keep the noun list primarily untouchable.

As I created this list, I had to decide what to do with the people nouns. Many important nouns and noun suffixes related to “what people do.” I don’t find that what a person does is touchable. Here’s how I see it:

  • That runner (noun) is fast.    (runner = that person = touchable)
  • He is a fast runner (noun).    (runner = what the person does is not touchable)

In short, you will find many PEOPLE nouns that relate to what people do.

Keep this in Mind: Words are Not a Part Of Speech until They Are Placed in a Sentence

We can use all of the words on the noun list as a noun in a sentence. However, we can also use many of the words as a different part of speech. Here are two simple examples:

1.  A panther is a cat.   (cat = noun)
2.  A panther is a member of the cat family.   (cat = functions as an adjective)

1.  The present is all we have! Take action now!    (present = noun)
2.  Who was present for the meeting?    (present = adjective)

Gerunds Not Included: Gerunds are Verb Forms that Function as Nouns

I did not include gerunds on this list, as it could easily add another thousand words or more. Be sure to check out my giant list of 3,250 verbs to get started exploring verbs and gerunds. Here are two examples of gerunds:

1.  Swimming is a fun and healthy activity.    (swimming = functions as a noun)
2.  My grandmother’s cooking is the best!    (cooking = functions as a noun)

Noun Suffixes: A Few Quick Notes

These 18 noun suffixes are the heart of this page. The list of 4,640 nouns has less value if you don’t understand the basics of suffixes and how suffixes can change a word from one part of speech to another. This is an essential aspect of understanding word morphology.

A few quick notes before you look over this list of 18 noun suffixes:

1.  Word Counts: Every suffix has a word count that tells you the total number of times that suffix appears on the giant list of 4,640 nouns. This word count will help you identify the most common and least important noun suffixes.

2.  Rules: These are not suffix rules. Most of the suffixes that follow also create words that are not nouns.

3.  Spelling Changes: I don’t address the various spelling rule changes that occur when adding suffixes.

4.  Syllables: You will see that I have divided many of the words in this suffix list to separate the suffix from the base. Please Note: I did not divide them by syllables.

5.  Complete Words: On this suffix list, I focused on adding the suffixes to complete words instead of Greek and Latin roots. In contrast, the giant list of 4,640 nouns comprises many different word structures.
