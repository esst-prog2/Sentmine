What Do Adverbs Do?
Please Note:
This page is devoted to (1) single-word adverbs. It does not address the other two types of adverbials: (2) adverbial phrases and (3) adverbial clauses.
Adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, and other adverbs. Adverbs answer these questions:
How? When? Where? To what extent or degree? How much? How often?
Please note: Single-word adverbs don’t answer the question “Why?”.
Syntax: Adverb Slots: Where Do Adverbs Go?
Syntax is the set of rules that guide where words belong in a sentence. In a sense, each part of speech has a syntactic slot.
Nouns go in the noun slot
;
adjectives in the adjective slot
; adverbs in the adverb slot, etc.
The adverb slot is the most MOVEABLE and REMOVABLE part of speech. In short, we can usually move adverbs or remove them, and the sentence will still make sense. Because adverbs are modifiers, they are
add-ons
—formally called
adjuncts
.

Keep in Mind: Same Adverb, Different Modification
Keep in mind that the same adverb can modify different parts of speech:
1.
Modifies a Verb:
The scientists
genetically
modified
the food. (modified = verb)
2.
Modifies an Adjective:
This is a
genetically
modified
food substance! (modified = adjective)
Keep in Mind: Same Word, Different Part of Speech
We can’t call a word a part of speech until it is in a sentence. Once it is in a sentence, we can identify what part of speech it is.
1. The kitten was so
cuddly
cute
. (cuddly = adverb) (cute = predicate adjective)
Note:
“Cuddly cute” should only be used in a narrative story because cuddly is not listed as an adverb in any dictionary. Still, it is in an adverb slot.
2. The kitten was very
cuddly
. (cuddly = predicate adjective)
1. She always
acted
neighborly
. (neighborly = adverb) (acted = verb)
Note:
Modern dictionaries don’t list “neighborly” as an adjective. In short, that means you can’t act neighborly, which makes this sentence grammatically incorrect. Still, it is in an adverb slot.
2. She always greeted me with a
neighborly
hello
. (neighborly = adjective) (hello = noun)
Remember:
It’s only an adverb if it functions as an adverb.
