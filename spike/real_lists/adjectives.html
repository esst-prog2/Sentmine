4,800 Adjectives: The Giant List of Adjectives

Below, you will find the largest list of adjectives you are likely to find anywhere! Small lists of adjectives treat the words on the list as the good or the only adjectives, and the words that are not on the list as less than desirable adjectives or possibly not even adjectives at all. In short, small lists of adjectives teach very little about the true nature of adjectives. This enormous list of adjectives will challenge your understanding of adjectives while catapulting your vocabulary to a new level.

But first, we must examine a few concepts to help us understand what we see in this list. We have three categories of words that are called or function as adjectives:

1.   True Adjectives / Descriptive Adjectives
2.   Determiners
3.   Participles / Participle Adjectives

By function as adjectives, I mean the words can fill an adjective syntactic slot. Traditional grammar still treats most kinds of determiners as adjectives, so I have also included them in this list. Participles are a highly effective and sadly neglected form of adjective. Truthfully, most participles do not fall into the category of True Adjectives; however, all of the participles on this list can be used as adjectives. In other words, all of them can be placed into at least one adjective slot.

The Adjective Slots: Attributive Adjectives and Predicate Adjectives

We have two types of adjective slots: 1) Attributive Adjectives and 2) Predicate Adjectives. We find the two slots in three locations:

1.   Attributive Adjective in the Subject:          The small child is happy.
2.   Attributive Adjective in the Predicate:       Jim is a small child.
3.   Predicate Adjective in the Predicate:        Sue is small.

Adjective Verification and Practice: The Test Sentences

Most students learn the same or similar adjectives year after year. But adjectives are not just beautiful—adjectives are also practical. We must expand and challenge our understanding of adjectives to grasp this fact, and spending time with this list does exactly that.

I created this list from scratch using several techniques and strategies. In the end, I had to verify that I could use each of these adjectives in an adjective slot, so I printed out this entire list and tested all of these adjectives in a few short sentences. It was an enlightening adjective experience!

You are bound to find at least a few words where you think, “We can’t use that word as an adjective!” For this reason, I’ve included the following sentences to help you verify for yourselves that all of these are, in fact, adjectives. Each of these short sentences contains at least one adjective slot. These slots are not random, as they are based on English syntax. In most cases, the only word that can fit in the slot is an adjective. Illustrating this fact is the word VERY, which is used in a few of the sentences. If you remove the word VERY from two of the sentences, a verb could also fill the slot. In these cases, the word VERY guarantees that the word that follows it is an adjective.

• The dog is ___.• That ___ idea is ___.• She sat next to the ___ NOUN.
• The ___ student is ___.• His ___ NOUN is ___.• He is very ___.
• Life is ___.• It was very ___.• She felt very ___.

The Key: 1, 2, and 3

On the list of 4,800 adjectives, a few special categories are marked with the following numbers: 1, 2, or 3. Let’s see what the numbers mean.

1.   (Total: 493) -ER and -EST: These words can have –er and –est added to the end of them. Adding these suffixes creates the Comparative and Superlative forms. Surprisingly, out of 4,800 adjectives, only about 500 accept –er and –est. Keep in mind that we can also place more and most in front of many adjectives to create Comparative and Superlative forms.   (See a complete explanation and a complete list of Comparative and Superlative Adjectives and Adverbs here.)

2.   (Total: 56) Determiners: You’ll see that determiners don’t fit into most of the sample sentences above. That’s what makes them determiners. It’s ridiculous that traditional grammar still classifies most determiners as “special adjectives.” Having said that, we should teach students about determiners when teaching them about adjectives.

3.   (Total: 53) Nouns as Adjectives: These are a type of determiner. This topic is undoubtedly a topic for further debate and discussion; however, it’s worth including a few of these on the list for discussion purposes. In one sense, we can place most nouns in front of another noun to create a noun functioning as an adjective. However, in most cases, it will create an informal or colloquial expression. However, most of the 53 listed nouns-as-adjectives on this list are extremely common.

A Note on The Danger of Word Lists:    We should be teaching students that one must analyze a word in a sentence in order to determine what part of speech it is. While it’s true that all of the following words CAN BE used as adjectives, many can also be used as nouns, verbs, etc. Put simply, these words ARE NOT adjectives—but they can all BE USED AS adjectives. There is a difference!
